About

01 / Executive Summary

An engineer who thinks like a systems integrator.

I'm a Mechanical Engineering junior at Colorado State University focused on aerospace propulsion and integrated vehicle systems. My engineering philosophy centers on the decision process behind a design as much as the part that ends up on the bench.

I treat each project as a sequence of trade studies: identify the constraint that matters most, justify the choice with data, and prototype fast enough to be wrong cheaply. That mindset runs through my SolidWorks and ANSYS workflows, my ESP32 firmware and PID tuning, my MATLAB and Simulink simulations, and my hands-on work in machining, 3D printing, and composites.

I document carefully because an engineering decision that cannot be communicated cannot be reviewed. What I bring to a team is a junior engineer who is already equally comfortable in a CAD assembly, on an electronics bench, and in a design review.

02 / Core Competencies

Three areas of working capability.

Systems Integration & Engineering Design

I treat mechanical, electronic, and control subsystems as one continuous design problem, using interface specifications and CAD-to-firmware traceability so every connection between subsystems is owned, documented, and testable.

Technical Leadership & Project Management

I lead by writing the spec everyone else builds against, scheduling design reviews around real decision points rather than calendar dates, and surfacing blockers early so the team spends its time on engineering instead of coordination.

Hands-On Prototyping & Manufacturing

I shorten the loop between concept and test article by selecting the right fabrication method for each part (FDM print, machined aluminum, composite layup, or sheet metal) and by building test rigs that measure what the design actually needs to be measured against.

03 / Philosophy

What I want my work to show.

Engineering judgment is built one documented decision at a time. I want my work to show not only what I designed, but why I designed it that way, what data convinced me, and what I would change if I had to do it again. The hardware on the bench is the proof. The reasoning behind it is the work.
